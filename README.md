While working on my bankruptcy predictor project, the biggest problem I ran into while structuring the data was ensuring the dataset would actually be usable for modeling. The dataset on Kaggle initially looked clean to me, especially when I completed milestone 1 with it, but in reality, the status_label column contained a mix of numeric and string values (0 and “failed”). It wasn’t until I was halfway through this assignment that I realized that this was causing a large problem, and I had to find a way to fix it. I carefully inspected the data and converted all the labels into a consistent numeric format (0 and 1). This step was extremely important because even small inconsistencies in the data can completely break a machine learning model. Another challenge I ran into was deciding which variables to include. The dataset contained many financial indicators, some of which overlapped or were less relevant, so I had to select a smaller set that captured firm size, profitability, and debt without overcomplicating the model. 

From a historical perspective, the results generally support my initial hypothesis that bankruptcy is influenced by multiple financial factors rather than one dominant variable, and that the economic troubles of the 2008 financial crisis had an impact on business and bankruptcies. The feature importance results show that variables like market value, revenue, and liabilities all play a role, with no single factor overwhelmingly determining outcomes. However, the comparison between pre- and post-2008 periods revealed something slightly surprising: while the overall structure remains stable, there are small shifts in which variables matter most. In particular, market-based and profitability measures appear to have become more important after 2008. This suggests that broader economic conditions, such as the financial crisis, may influence how bankruptcy risk is evaluated. Overall, the data support a new view of bankruptcy while also pointing to the importance of historical context.
